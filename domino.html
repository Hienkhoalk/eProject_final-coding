Domino

To play dominoes, players take turns placing domino tiles on the table, matching the number of dots on one end of a played tile to an open end on the table, building a chain of tiles; the first player to play all their dominoes wins, with the goal of strategically playing tiles to block opponents and create opportunities to play your own tiles

                Key points about playing dominoes:
	Domino set:
    A standard domino set consists of 28 tiles, with each tile having two sides displaying the same number of dots (from 0 to 6). 
	Starting the game:
        •	Players draw tiles from a shuffled pile until everyone has the same number of tiles. 
        •	The player with the highest double (e.g., double six) starts by placing that tile on the table. 
	Playing a turn:
        •	Each player takes a turn to play a domino tile that matches the number of dots on either open end of the existing chain on the table. 
        •	If a player cannot play a tile, they must draw another from the stockpile until they can play. 
	Ending the game:
        •	The first player to play all their tiles wins the round. 
    Scoring (depending on the variation):
	Basic scoring:
    The winner of a round may receive points based on the total pips left in the hands of the other players. 
        •	"All Fives" or "Muggins":
    Players score points when the sum of the open ends on the table is a multiple of five. 
    Important strategies:
        •	Play your doubles early: This can help control the flow of the game and limit opponent options. 
        •	Analyze your hand: Consider the tiles you have and plan your plays to strategically block opponents. 
        •	Watch the open ends: Pay attention to the numbers on the open ends of the chain to identify opportunities to play your tiles.
